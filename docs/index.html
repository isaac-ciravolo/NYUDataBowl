Methodology & sources

Relative score (1–100). Within a frozen peer cohort (same position group & metric for players; same exact rusher count & treatment definition for teams), each metric is oriented so larger is more favorable, tied values receive ascending average ranks, and the score is 1 + 99 × (rank − 1)/(N − 1) where N is the cohort size. Rounding is for display only. The cohort is frozen before any row is selected, so picking a row never changes its score. Scores are suppressed for sparse rows, missing comparisons, or cohorts with fewer than 10 eligible entities; if every value ties, the metric shows “No differentiation.” Aggregate results with no peer distribution (Overview, Stay-or-Go) are never scored and stay in actual units even in score mode.

Favorability. Lower chip release delay is more favorable; greater depth retention and greater separation gain are more favorable; a more negative chip-minus-no-chip pressure difference is more favorable.

Confidence intervals stay in their original units from the source bootstrap; there are no score CIs. Actual values, units, sample counts, CIs, and missingness remain available via columns and hover tooltips in every mode.

Interpretation guardrails. Within-rusher and play-level pressure answer different questions (one rusher's own rate vs. the whole play). Timing-window retention (share of actual throws inside 2–5 s) differs from timing-data availability (share of plays with any timing record). Known no-throw exclusions (sack/run) differ from route features missing from the parquet. Receiving separation on go plays is observed separation when backs release — not the counterfactual receiving cost of staying. Team comparisons are observational and often rest on very few games. Larger separation alone does not establish greater receiving value.
