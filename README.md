# The Dual-Duty Tax: Pricing the TE/RB Chip

**[View the interactive dashboard](#)** 
(link to be added) for the chip-versus-clean route comparison.

When a tight end or running back chips a rusher before releasing into his route, what does that block cost the passing game and what does it buy back? We treat each player as his own control and compare his chip routes against his own clean routes to isolate the assignment from talent, though chips are called on slower-developing plays and against dangerous rushers, so with only Weeks 1 to 8 of 2021 and thin per-player samples these are observed differences rather than causal proof. Using tracking and PFF data, we measure release time and route depth from normalized player positions, pressure from PFF rusher outcomes, and targets parsed from play descriptions, with bootstrap confidence intervals over players and rushers. The chip costs a tight end 1.3 seconds of release and 3.8 yards of depth, but it drops the chipped rusher's pressure rate from 11.4% to 7.5% and turns the route into a dependable checkdown that, for tight ends, completes 88% of the time when targeted versus 69% on clean routes. A coordinator can weigh that trade when deciding who to keep in to block, and a scout can use it to profile dual-threat tight ends and backs by how much route value they give up for the protection they add.
